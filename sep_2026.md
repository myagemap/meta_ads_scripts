# MyAgeMAP — Meta Ads Script Pack
**Objective:** App Installs (Play Store + App Store)
**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 30 seconds each
**Placements:** Reels, Stories, Feed
**Language:** Hinglish VO + burned-in English captions
**Reusable ending:** Seconds 25–30 are IDENTICAL across all three. Only the store link in the CTA button changes.

## SCRIPT 1 — "Das Minute" (Ten Minutes)
### Parenting & Energy · Male, 34 · IT Professional

### Characters

**ARJUN, 34 — IT professional, Hyderabad.**
A soft-spoken product manager who still owns his college cricket bat but hasn't swung it in four years; the kind of man who thinks he's "basically fine" because he's never been to a hospital.
Cast someone with a slight desk-slouch and a real (not gym-built) body — relatability beats aspiration here.

**VIHAAN, 8 — Arjun's son.**
Endless-battery kid who has been asking his father to bowl to him every evening for two weeks and finally got a yes.
Cast a genuinely energetic child; his stamina is the visual benchmark the father is measured against — he is the "symbol" of the ad.

### Setting / Background
Apartment gated-community play area at golden hour, 6:00–6:30 pm. Concrete pitch, painted compound walls, a few parked two-wheelers, other kids faintly audible off-frame. Warm low sun, long shadows. Strictly an everyday Indian middle-class society — **not** a stadium, **not** a manicured lawn.

### Props / Items
Old tennis ball · plastic bat · three plastic stumps or a chair as wicket · laptop bag dropped on the ground with the office ID card still clipped on · a bottle of water · his phone face-down on the bag.

### Script

| Time | Shot | Audio / On-screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **HOOK.** Tight on Arjun bent over, hands on knees, breathing hard. Cut wide — he's been playing for ten minutes. | SFX: heavy breathing. <br> Text: **10 minutes. That's all it took.** |
| 2–6s | Rewind feel: Arjun arrives home, loosens his tie, drops the laptop bag. Vihaan runs in holding the bat. Arjun smiles, rolls his sleeves. | VO (Arjun, internal): "Beta do saal se keh raha tha. Aaj maine haan bol diya." <br> Text: *He'd been asking for two years.* |
| 6–12s | Fun montage — Arjun bowls, Vihaan hits, both laugh. Genuine joy. Handheld, close, warm. | Music lifts. Natural laughter. |
| 12–16s | The turn. Arjun slows. Wipes his forehead. Reaches for water. Vihaan, completely unaffected, calls out for one more ball. | Vihaan: "Papa, ek aur!" <br> Arjun: "Beta… bas do minute." <br> Text: *The kid wasn't tired. He was.* |
| 16–20s | Arjun sits on the step, watching his son play alone. Quiet. Reflective. Not sad — thoughtful. | VO: "Main 34 ka hoon. Toh thakaan 34 ki kyun nahi lagti?" <br> Text: **34 on paper. But how old is the body?** |
| 20–25s | He picks up his phone. Opens MyAgeMAP. Answers a couple of questions with his thumb. Report loads. His face changes — he leans in. | VO: "Calendar age sirf ek number hai. Body ki apni age hoti hai." <br> Text: **Your body keeps its own age.** |
| 25–30s | **SHARED CLOSING BLOCK** | |

## SCRIPT 2 — "Aath Ghante" (Eight Hours)
### Desk Fatigue & Sleep · Female, 29 · Mid-level Working Professional

### Characters

**PRIYA, 29 — Marketing manager at a mid-size company.**
Competent, mid-level, four years into the role, does everything right on paper — eats okay, sleeps eight hours, hits the occasional yoga class — and is still running on fumes by Wednesday.
**Casting note (per your brief): strictly a mid-level working professional — not a founder, not an intern.** Her lanyard, her shared desk and her second-hand laptop should say "employee", not "boss". She is the symbol of every salaried woman in a 9-to-7 Indian office.

**MEERA, 30 — Priya's teammate, one desk away.**
Same job, same hours, same commute — and visibly has energy left at 6 pm, which is the whole point of her being in frame.
She never speaks about health; she is purely a **visual contrast device**. Cast someone with relaxed posture and a brighter, more awake presence.

### Setting / Background
Two locations.
1. **Bedroom, 6:45 am** — small Indian city apartment. Cotton bedsheet, mosquito net or curtain, alarm on a bedside table, thin morning light through a grille window.
2. **Open-plan office, Bengaluru/Hyderabad style** — cluster desks, a pantry corner, glass partition, other people working. Cool white light against the bedroom's warm light.

### Props / Items
Phone alarm showing **8h 12m sleep** on a sleep-tracker screen · three used coffee cups building up through the day on her desk · smartwatch on her wrist · office lanyard · sticky notes · a dupatta on the chair back.

### Script

| Time | Shot | Audio / On-screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **HOOK.** Close on a sleep-tracker screen: **8h 12m.** Pull back — Priya is still lying there, eyes open, exhausted. | Text: **8 hours of sleep. Still tired.** |
| 2–6s | She sits up slowly. Rubs her eyes. Ties her hair. Goes through the motions of getting ready without energy. | VO (Priya, internal): "Poori neend. Phir bhi subah se thakaan." <br> Text: *Full sleep. Zero rest.* |
| 6–11s | Office. Coffee cup #1 at 10 am. Cup #2 at 1 pm. Cup #3 at 4 pm — quick match-cut on the same desk corner, time-of-day light shifting. | VO: "Chai, coffee, phir coffee. Phir bhi 4 baje dimaag band." <br> Text: *Three coffees by 4 pm.* |
| 11–15s | 6 pm. Priya slumped, screen light on her face. Rack focus to Meera one desk away — packing up, stretching, laughing with someone, full of energy. | Text: **Same hours. Same desk. Different energy. Why?** |
| 15–20s | Priya notices. Not jealous — curious. She looks down at her own smartwatch, then at her phone. | VO: "Problem neend ki nahi thi. Problem kahin aur thi." <br> Text: *The problem wasn't the sleep.* |
| 20–25s | She opens MyAgeMAP. Connects her smartwatch — Apple Health / Google Fit sync animation. Sleep and heart-rate data flow in. Report builds on screen. | VO: "Watch connect ki. Report mili. Sab clear ho gaya." <br> Text: **Connect your watch. Get your report.** |
| 25–30s | **SHARED CLOSING BLOCK** | |

## SCRIPT 3 — "Subah Ki Sair" (The Morning Walk)
### Post-40 Active Life · Male, 46 · Grandparent

### Characters

**RAMESH, 46 — bank employee, walks in the park every single morning.**
Has not missed his 6 am walk in eleven years and is quietly proud of it, which makes the new stiffness in his knee feel less like a symptom and more like a betrayal.
Cast a fit-looking but real 46 — salt-and-pepper at the temples, walking shoes that have actually been used. He must look **disciplined**, not unhealthy; the insight lands harder when the audience can't blame him.

**AARADHYA, 4 — Ramesh's granddaughter.**
Meets him at the end of every walk and always wants to be picked up and carried the last stretch home.
She is the **symbol of the ad**: the moment he hesitates before lifting her is the entire emotional beat. Cast a small, light child and a natural, affectionate dynamic.

> **Note on casting a 46-year-old grandparent:** this works in India but reads slightly young on camera. If the shoot feels off, keep Ramesh at 46 and simply age the character up visually, or swap Aaradhya to a 12-year-old grandchild he walks with instead of lifts. The script beat survives either way.

### Setting / Background
A large public morning park — Lalbagh / KBR / Necklace Road energy. Banyan tree, mud-and-stone walking track, laughter-club group in the far background, other seniors in white walking shoes, morning mist and low golden light. Deeply familiar, deeply Indian. No gym, no treadmill.

### Props / Items
Steel water bottle · small towel around the neck · worn walking shoes · fitness band or basic smartwatch · park bench · his phone in a pouch at his waist.

### Script

| Time | Shot | Audio / On-screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2s | **HOOK.** Low angle on walking shoes hitting the track — strong, rhythmic. Then one step falters. He stops. Hand goes to his knee. | Text: **11 years. Never missed a morning walk.** |
| 2–7s | Wide: Ramesh on the park track at dawn, other walkers overtaking him. He used to be the one overtaking. | VO (Ramesh, internal): "Gyarah saal se ek din nahi chhoda." <br> Text: *He did everything right.* |
| 7–12s | He sits on the bench earlier than usual. Checks his band: fewer steps than his normal. Rolls his shoulder. Rubs the knee again. | VO: "Par ab do round ke baad hi ghutna jawab de deta hai." <br> Text: *Two rounds, and the knee gives up.* |
| 12–17s | Aaradhya runs up to him at the park gate, arms out, wanting to be lifted. Ramesh reaches — then **hesitates**. A half-second pause. He lifts her anyway, but we saw it. | Aaradhya: "Thatha, uppy!" <br> Text: **He'd never hesitated before.** |
| 17–21s | Holding her, walking home, he looks at his own hand on her back. Thoughtful. | VO: "Umar 46 hai. Lekin sharir kitne saal ka hai?" <br> Text: **46 on paper. And the body?** |
| 21–25s | Bench. He opens MyAgeMAP. Answers the lifestyle questions. Report generates — score, then personalised recommendations scrolling. | VO: "Report aayi. Kya karna hai, sab likha tha." <br> Text: **Know it. Then work on it.** |
| 25–30s | **SHARED CLOSING BLOCK** | |

## Production Notes (all three)

**The 2-second rule.** Meta Reels are won or lost in the first two seconds. Every hook above opens on the *consequence*, not the setup — the breathing, the 8h12m, the faltering step. Do not add a logo intro. Do not add a slow establishing shot.

**Burned-in captions are mandatory.** The large majority of Reels play on mute. Every line of VO above must also appear as on-screen text in a bold, high-contrast font, positioned in the middle-upper third so the Instagram/Facebook UI doesn't cover it.

**Same ending, same shoot.** Shoot the closing block once with one clean phone and one pair of hands. Re-using identical footage across three creatives also makes your A/B test cleaner — the only variable is the story, not the CTA.

**Vary the thumbnail, not the ending.** Since the ends are identical, the differentiation has to happen at the top. Three distinct opening frames = three distinct scroll-stop rates to compare.

## Meta Ad Policy — read this before writing the ad copy

This is the part that most often gets health-app ads rejected, so it's worth getting right the first time.

**Do not imply you know something about the viewer.** Meta's Personal Attributes policy prohibits ad text that asserts or implies knowledge of a person's health, condition or physical state. In practice:

- ❌ "You're aging faster than you think"
- ❌ "Your body is older than 34"
- ❌ "Feeling tired all the time?"
- ✅ "Many Indians in their 30s are surprised by their biological age"
- ✅ "Know your biological age"
- ✅ "Arjun's calendar said 34. His report said something else."

Third-person and general framing is safe. Second-person accusation is not. Note that this restriction applies to the **ad copy and on-screen text** — the story inside the video showing a character's own experience is fine.

**Avoid medical claims.** MyAgeMAP produces a wellness report, not a diagnosis. Keep away from "diagnose", "treat", "cure", "reverse aging", "detect disease". "Personalised insights", "know your biological age", "lifestyle recommendations" are all fine.

**Before/after imagery is restricted** for health and body-related ads. None of these three scripts use it — keep it that way.
